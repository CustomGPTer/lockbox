Privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026. This policy covers the LockBox app for Android (package uk.jj.lockbox) and this website. LockBox is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • LockBox does not have Android's internet permission. It cannot send anything from your phone, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • LockBox has no accounts and no servers. The developer receives nothing from your phone and cannot see, open or recover what you store.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Everything LockBox keeps is on your phone, encrypted, in storage that other apps cannot read. It is deleted when you uninstall the app.

What stays on your phone

LockBox keeps the following in its private storage:

  • The files you add, and a small preview picture of each, encrypted.
  • An encrypted index holding file names, folders, tags, notes and cards.
  • The key that opens the vault, stored only in locked form: locked with your PIN or password, with your recovery code, and, if you turn it on, with a key released by your fingerprint. Your PIN, password and recovery code are never stored.
  • The break-in log: the time of each wrong try and, if you switched it on, a front-camera photo. Entries are sealed so that they can be read only after you unlock.
  • Your settings, and a count of wrong tries so that the waits can be enforced.

None of this is included in Android's backup or in a transfer to a new phone. The only copy that leaves the app is a backup file, and only when you make one and choose where it goes.

LockBox does not ask for the internet, your contacts, your location, your microphone, your messages or access to all your files.

Services the app contacts

None. LockBox has no internet permission and contacts nothing. When you tap Open in browser on the updates screen, LockBox asks your browser to open the download page on GitHub; what happens then is between your browser and GitHub, as described under This website.

Other people

The photo taken after a wrong try may capture someone other than you, and the files you store may be about other people. Both stay on your phone and are yours to manage. You are responsible for how you use them, and for telling people who share your phone that it is protected in this way if the law where you live expects that.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • A file you delete goes to LockBox's recycle bin for 30 days and is then removed. You can empty the bin at any time.
  • The break-in log has a Clear button.
  • Settings › Delete this LockBox destroys the keys and then every stored file.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling LockBox deletes everything it holds.
  • A backup file you have made is an ordinary file wherever you saved it. Delete it there when you no longer want it.
  • Nothing is held anywhere else, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of LockBox holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What LockBox stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

Files, previews and the index are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with a separate key for each file. Your PIN or password is stretched with Argon2id and is never stored. The key that opens the vault is also locked with a key held in the phone's security hardware. Because LockBox cannot go online, nothing it holds can be taken over a network through the app.

No software is free of faults, and LockBox has not had an independent security review. It cannot protect an open vault from software with full control of a rooted phone. The limits are set out on the home page.

This website

This site sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the app file is downloaded from GitHub Releases. GitHub, as the host, receives the technical details any web server sees, such as your IP address and browser type, and handles them under the GitHub privacy statement. The developer does not receive them.

Children

LockBox is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone. A parent or guardian should decide whether a child uses it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version is published here.

Contact

For a question about privacy or your data, open an issue on GitHub. Please do not put private details in an issue; it is public.
